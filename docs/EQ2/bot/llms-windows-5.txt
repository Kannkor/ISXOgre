# Lag

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Records every frame of the last few minutes - how long it took and what ran in it - so when your FPS drops you can open that moment and see why.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **The line** is your FPS, one point a second, for the last 3 minutes. A red tick marks a second with a frame over the budget (33 ms = under 30 FPS).
> - **Dips** lists those moments, newest first, with what caused the worst frame: **OgreBot**, the **GPU** (the game waiting on the graphics card), or **the game** itself. Click one to open its frames.
> - **What adds to the dips** ranks OgreBot's calls by how much longer they run in a dip than in a normal frame, and how often they run.
> - **Stats over** picks the window the numbers at the top use (30 s, 1 min, 5 min).
> - **Save frames** writes every recorded frame to a file under `OgreSave\EQ2\OgreBot\` to send to us.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - It is always recording, even with this window closed. Its own cost is `ogre lag overhead`.
> - The target is: 95% of frames under 1.5 x your typical frame, and under 2% of frames over 33 ms.
> - `ogre lag report` prints the same numbers to the console; `ogre lag off` stops recording.
> - It holds the last 5 minutes by default. `ogre lag minutes 30` holds 30 (about 23 MB) - use it before a long session you want to save.

**Open it:** Type `ogre lag`. `ogre end lag` (or `ogre lag close`) closes it. If the window is already open, typing `ogre lag` again does nothing.

## What it is for

A recorder for frame rate drops. The bot writes down every frame the game draws - how long it took, how much of that time was the bot's own work, and which parts of the bot ran in it - and keeps the last few minutes. When your FPS drops, open this window and click that moment to see the frames around it and what caused the slow one: OgreBot, the graphics card, or the game itself. It records all the time, even with the window closed, so you can look after the drop has happened. Most players only need it when the game stutters and they want to know whether the bot is the reason.

## The numbers at the top

Read-only. All of these use the period picked under Stats over (1 minute to start with). Frames while zoning or on a load screen are left out.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **FPS (the big number)** | Your average frames per second over the period. |
| **median, 95%, worst** | How long frames took, in milliseconds. Median is a typical frame. 95% means 95 frames in 100 were faster than this; the number in brackets is that time divided by the median (1.00x would mean every frame is even). Worst is the slowest single frame. |
| **over 33.3 ms** | The share of frames slower than 33.3 ms - that is, frames that drop you under 30 FPS. These slow frames are what this window calls dips. |
| **frame-to-frame** | How much one frame's time differs from the next, on average. A low number means smooth play, even if the FPS is not high. |
| **target met / target not met** | Green when the period was smooth: 95 frames in 100 took less than 1.5 times the median frame, and fewer than 2 in 100 frames were over 33.3 ms. Red when either is not true. Not shown when there are no frames yet. |
| **OgreBot: … ms of each frame** | How much of each frame, on average, was the bot's own work: all parts of the bot, drawing its windows, and any bot commands that ran between frames. Then the period and how many frames it covers. |
| **Warning line (yellow)** | Shows 'Recording is OFF' when recording is turned off. It can also say the window system loaded in your game is too old to report its time - the UI and GPU numbers then read 0 until you restart the game client. |

## Stats over, Recording and Save frames

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Stats over: 30 s / 1 min / 5 min** | Picks the period used by the numbers at the top, the counts under Dips, the What adds to the dips list, and the 'typical frame' used to judge what caused each dip. The picked button is outlined. Display only - changes what you see, not what the bot does.<br>*The FPS line and the list of dips always cover the last 3 minutes, whichever button is picked.* | 1 min |
| **Recording** | Turns the recorder on or off. Off: nothing new is recorded, but the frames already recorded are kept. The choice is saved and applies to every character on this PC.<br>*While Recording is off the window stops updating, even after you click something. Close and reopen it to see the current state.* | on |
| **Save frames** | Saves every frame the recorder is holding (the last 5 minutes, unless you changed that) to a file in OgreSave/EQ2/OgreBot/, named lag- plus the session name and the date and time. The console says how many frames were saved and where. Send the file to us when you report lag.<br>*Saving takes a moment, so the frame you saved in shows up as a dip itself. The console line says how long it took.* |  |

## The FPS line

Read-only. Your FPS, one point a second, for the last 3 minutes - newest on the right. Grey lines mark every 20 FPS. A red tick along the bottom marks a second that had a frame over 33.3 ms; the taller the tick, the more slow frames in that second. Hover a tick to see how many and the worst one.

## Dips

Every slow moment of the last 3 minutes, newest first. Slow frames that come within a tenth of a second of each other are one entry. The line at the top counts the slow frames in the Stats over period and how many each cause had. When the dips come at a steady beat it adds 'they repeat every … ms' and how many of the gaps match - a steady beat usually means something on a timer.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Click a dip** | Opens the frames around that dip (see below). Each entry shows how long ago it was, the cause of its worst frame, the biggest bot call in that frame with its time, the worst frame's time, and 'x2', 'x3' … when more than one frame was slow. |

## What adds to the dips

Read-only. The bot's calls over the Stats over period - up to 14 - with the one that adds most to the slow frames first. A call that runs no longer in a slow frame than in a normal one is listed after those, by how long it takes on average. Hover a row for more: how often it ran in slow and in normal frames, its average time per frame, and how long it takes 95 times in 100.

## The frames around a dip

Opens over the two lists when you click a dip. One row per frame, from half a second before the dip to a second and a half after it. Slow frames are bright and carry their cause. 'typical frame' at the top is the median frame of the Stats over period.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Back** | Closes the frame list and shows the two lists again. |
| **Title bar close (X)** | Closes the window. The recorder keeps recording. |

## What the window shows

- Dip causes: OgreBot (red) - the bot's own work covers at least half of the extra time over a typical frame. GPU/Present (yellow) - the game handing its picture to the graphics card covers at least half of it. game/other (grey) - neither: the game itself, or something else on the PC the recorder cannot see.
- What adds to the dips - +… ms: how much longer the call runs in a slow frame than in a normal one, on average. A minus means it runs shorter in slow frames.
- What adds to the dips - % of dips: in how many of the slow frames the call ran. worst: its slowest single run, in ms. every … ms: shown when it runs on a steady timer.
- (command) in front of a name: an `ogre` command or a script call into the bot (for example one sent from another session), not part of the bot's regular work each frame.
- (inside) in front of a name: a step that runs inside another call on the list, so its time is already part of that call's time.
- Frame list - when: seconds from the start of the dip. frame: how long the frame took, in ms.
- Frame list - OgreBot: all of the bot's time in that frame (adapter + core + UI + cmds). adapter: the EverQuest 2 part of the bot. core: the shared part of the bot (with '(… panel)' when clicks from the bot's windows were handled in that frame). UI: drawing the bot's windows. cmds: bot commands that ran between frames.
- Frame list - GPU: time the game spent handing its picture to the graphics card. reads: how many values the bot read from the game in that frame. biggest calls: the bot's slowest calls in that frame, up to 8, in ms.
- Calls that take less than 0.01 ms in a frame are not named for that frame - their time is still counted in OgreBot's total.
- The window updates itself about once a second while you are in the game.

## Good to know

- It is always recording, even with the window closed. `ogre lag off` (or unticking Recording) stops it.
- It holds the last 5 minutes by default. `ogre lag minutes 30` holds 30 (about 23 MB of memory) - do that before a long session you want to save. Changing it throws away what was already recorded.
- A dip marked GPU/Present or game/other had no bot work big enough to explain it. Graphics settings, other programs or the game itself are the better place to look.
- The list of dips always covers 3 minutes, but the count above it covers only the Stats over period - so the count can be smaller than the list.
- Recording, the budgets and the minutes to hold are saved to OgreSave/EQ2/OgreBot/lag.json and apply to every character on this PC. Stats over is not saved - it is back to 1 min after the bot is reloaded.

Opened while Recording is off, the frames already recorded are still shown, with a warning above the buttons:

![Recording off](../assets/lag/off.png)

A calm stretch - no frame over 33.3 ms in the last 3 minutes. The list on the right then simply shows the bot's costliest calls:

![No dips](../assets/lag/empty.png)

Console: `ogre lag report` prints the same numbers for the last minute (`ogre lag report 300` for 5 minutes). `ogre lag dump` is the Save frames button. `ogre lag clear` throws away what was recorded. `ogre lag overhead` prints what the recorder itself costs. `ogre lag budget <ms> <ms>` changes the limits: the second number is the 33.3 ms that marks a dip, and it must be larger than the first (25 ms to start with; the first number is not shown in this window).

# Migration Assistant

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Copies your OgreBot Classic settings into the new bot: logins, Inventory Manager settings and lists, OgreBot profiles, IRC settings, MCP buttons and saved AutoTarget lists. It also adds an InnerSpace game profile, **Everquest 2 ISXOgreX**, under each InnerSpace game that runs EverQuest2.exe (whatever you named it) - launch a session with it and the new bot loads by itself.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Nothing, usually** - at load, anything not copied yet is copied for you, and this window shows the progress, then closes.
> - **Check the status** of each item with `ogre migrate`: Migrated, No source found, Nothing to migrate, or Not migrated (a copy that failed - the message says why).
> - **Re-run (overwrite)** copies an item again from Classic, replacing what the new bot has. It asks first: **Overwrite** or **Cancel**.
> - **Add what is missing**, on the InnerSpace profile item, adds the profile to any game that lacks it and replaces nothing. It asks first: **Add** or **Cancel**.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Your Classic files are only read, never changed.
> - The InnerSpace profile is the one thing it adds outside the bot: a copy of your EverQuest 2 Client profile whose startup runs `timedcommand 10 ext isxogrex`. Your other profiles are not touched, and a backup of InnerSpace's GameConfiguration.XML is kept next to it (GameConfiguration.before-ISXOgreX.XML).
> - Each item is copied automatically only once. After that, only Re-run (overwrite) copies it again.
> - Problems are listed in ISXOgreEQ2.log.

**Open it:** Type `ogre migrate`. It also opens by itself once, a few seconds after the bot loads, when it finds Classic settings that have not been copied yet.

## What it is for

The Migration Assistant copies your Classic settings into the new bot: logins, Inventory Manager settings and lists, OgreBot profiles, IRC settings, MCP buttons and saved AutoTarget lists. It also adds an InnerSpace game profile, **Everquest 2 ISXOgreX**, that loads the new bot by itself. You do not have to do anything. When the bot loads, every item that has Classic files but nothing in the new bot yet is copied automatically, and this window pops up to show the progress, then closes itself. Your old Classic files are only read, never changed. Open it yourself with `ogre migrate` to see the status of each item and to copy an item again, replacing what the new bot has.

## The item boxes

Each box shows one thing that can be copied from Classic and where it stands right now. The status is worked out from your files every time the window opens.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Re-run (overwrite)** | Asks to copy this item from your Classic files again, replacing what the new bot already has. Nothing is copied yet - the box changes to an Overwrite / Cancel question first.<br>*Only shown when you opened the window with `ogre migrate`, and only for items that have Classic files to copy. The InnerSpace profile item has Add what is missing here instead.* |
| **Add what is missing** | Asks to add the InnerSpace profile to any EverQuest 2 game that lacks it. Nothing is replaced: a game that has it is left alone. Nothing is added yet - the box changes to an Add / Cancel question first.<br>*Only shown when you opened the window with `ogre migrate`.* |
| **Overwrite** | Copies the item again from your Classic files and replaces what the new bot has. For profiles, every copied profile file is rewritten. When it finishes, the box shows what was copied.<br>*Anything you changed in the new bot for that item since the first copy is lost.* |
| **Cancel** | Backs out of the Overwrite question. Nothing is copied. |
| **Title bar close (X)** | Closes the window. Copying that is already running keeps going.<br>*The automatic progress view closes by itself about 4 seconds after everything is done.* |



## Asking to overwrite

After Re-run (overwrite), the box asks before it replaces anything. This is the only question the assistant ever asks.

## The automatic run at load

When the bot loads and finds Classic files for an item that the new bot does not have yet, it copies them without asking and shows this view. There are no buttons here. Profiles can take a moment for a big library and show Running... until they are done. When every item is finished the window closes by itself.

## What the window shows

- ✓ Migrated (green) - the new bot already has this item. It may have been copied just now, automatically, or in an earlier session.
- No source found (grey) - you have no Classic files for this item, so there is nothing to copy.
- Nothing to migrate (grey) - you have Classic files for this item but the new bot has nothing yet, and no copy was tried this session.
- Not migrated (red) - this session tried to copy the item and could not. The message above it says why.
- Running... - the item is being done right now (OgreBot profiles and the InnerSpace profile only).
- Green message - what was copied, e.g. 'Migrated 6 characters to the new store.'
- Orange message - a warning or a question, e.g. a few profile files were skipped, or 'overwrite it?'.
- Red message - the copy failed. The details are in ISXOgreEQ2.log.
- Grey message - the item is still being copied (OgreBot profiles, which can take a moment).

## Good to know

- Your Classic files are never changed or deleted. You can keep using Classic alongside.
- An item is copied automatically only once: as soon as the new bot has that item's file, it is treated as done. To copy it again you must use Re-run (overwrite).
- Opening the window with `ogre migrate` first copies anything that is still waiting, without asking - the same as the automatic run.
- Profiles: files that were missing or could not be read are skipped and counted in the orange message. Running it again copies the same set, so re-running will not fix them - check ISXOgreEQ2.log for which files.
- Inventory Manager lists (sell, destroy, restock) go into your own custom files. A list you already have is kept, not replaced, unless you use Re-run (overwrite).
- Saved AutoTarget lists go into one file per zone. A list you already have in the new bot is kept, and any others are added beside it. Re-run (overwrite) replaces only the lists with the same name, and never removes one.
- The InnerSpace profile is the one thing added outside the bot: a copy of your EverQuest 2 profile, under each InnerSpace game that runs EverQuest2.exe, whose startup loads the new bot. Launch a session with it and the new bot loads by itself. Your other profiles are not touched, and a backup of GameConfiguration.XML is kept next to it. Its Add what is missing button adds the profile to any game that lacks it and changes nothing else.
- The first time the bot loads, the New User Tour runs first. The automatic copy starts a few seconds after the tour is finished or skipped.
- Typing `ogre migrate` while the window is open does nothing.

# New User Tour

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> A quick hands-on tour of the new Ogre windows. Each step moves on by itself as soon as you do it.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Step 1** - drag the right edge, bottom edge or bottom-right corner to resize the window.
> - **Step 2** - right-click the title bar to open its menu.
> - **Step 3** - choose **Save Position** so the window opens in the same spot next time.
> - **Step 4** - click the gear to open the settings, then click it again (or its X) to close them.
> - **Skip** ends the tour at any step. **Got it** closes it at the end.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Your other Ogre windows are hidden during the tour and come back when it ends.
> - Type `ogre tour` to see it again any time.

**Open it:** Opens by itself the first time the new bot loads. Type `ogre tour` to see it again any time (typing it while the tour is open does nothing).

## What it is for

A short hands-on tour of the new Ogre windows, for players coming from Classic. It starts with a short cartoon (the Welcome to OgreBot window), then teaches four things you do on the tour's own window: resize it, right-click its title bar, save its position, and open its settings gear. Each step moves on by itself as soon as you do it, and a pulsing outline shows you where to click. While the tour is up, your other Ogre windows are hidden; they come back when it ends.

## Step 1 - Resize me

Drag the right edge, the bottom edge or the bottom-right corner of the tour window (only those can be dragged). They pulse to show where. As soon as the size changes, the tour moves to step 2.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Skip** | Ends the tour now. It counts as seen, so it will not open by itself again. Your hidden windows come back.<br>*Every step has Skip. `ogre tour` brings the tour back later.* |
| **Title bar close (X)** | Closing the tour window is the same as Skip: the tour counts as seen and your hidden windows come back. |

## Step 2 - Right-click my title bar

Right-click the title bar (the bar with the window's name). Its menu opens and the tour moves to step 3. Every Ogre window with a title bar has this menu.

## Step 3 - Save this window's spot

In the title-bar menu, choose Save Position (it pulses). That makes the window open in the same place and size next time. Once you save, the tour moves to step 4.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Save Position (title-bar menu)** | Saves where this window is and how big it is, so it opens there next time. On the tour this also finishes step 3.<br>*If the menu closes without saving, right-click the title bar again.* |

## Step 4 - Open the settings

Click the gear (⚙) in the top-right corner. Many windows keep their settings behind a gear like this. Close the settings again (gear or its X) to finish.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **⚙ (Settings)** | Opens and closes a small example settings panel inside the tour window. On step 4, closing it finishes the step.<br>*The example settings do nothing and save nothing.* |
| **x (close settings)** | Closes the example settings panel, the same as clicking the gear again. |
| **A toggle option / A slider / A colour** | Examples only, to show what a settings panel looks like. Nothing here saves or changes anything. |

## All set

The last page reminds you that most commands work like before (`ogre` still loads OgreBot), and that right-clicking windows and lists often shows extra options.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Got it** | Closes the tour. It counts as seen and will not open by itself again. Your hidden windows come back. |

## What the window shows

- Step label - which step you are on (Step 1 of 4 to Step 4 of 4, then All set).
- Pulsing yellow outline - the thing to click or drag for the current step (the right and bottom edges and the bottom-right corner, the title bar, the Save Position menu row, or the gear).

## Good to know

- While the tour is open, every other Ogre window is hidden so you can focus. They all come back when the tour ends (Got it, Skip or closing it).
- If the InnerSpace console is open when the tour starts, it is closed so it does not push the tour out of the way.
- On the very first load, the Migration Assistant waits for the tour: your Classic settings are copied a few seconds after you finish or skip the tour.
- Whether you have seen the tour is saved in OgreSave\EQ2\TourState.json. Delete that file to have the tour open by itself again.

# EQ2Codex API Token

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Where you give the bot your eq2codex.com API token. It ties the Codex tabs' uploads to your account.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Get a token at eq2codex.com** opens the site in your browser. Sign in, then go to Settings, API Tokens.
> - **Paste your token** in the box.
> - **Save token** saves it, replacing any old token, and closes the window. Enter does the same.
> - **Close** leaves without saving.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Your saved token is never shown here, only whether one is set.
> - If a save fails, the window stays open, says why in red, and keeps your token in the box.

**Open it:** In OgreBot, open the **Codex Inspect** or **Codex Toons** tab and click **Setup API Token**.

## What it is for

This is where you give the bot your eq2codex.com API token. The Codex tabs upload character gear and raid rosters to eq2codex.com, and the token is what ties those uploads to your account. You create the token on the website (Settings → API Tokens), paste it here and save. The window never shows your saved token - only whether one is set and how long it is.

## Pasting your token

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Paste your token** | Paste the API token you created on eq2codex.com. Pressing Enter in the box is the same as Save token.<br>*The token leaves the screen with the window when a save works. If a save fails, the token stays in the box so you can try again.* | empty |



## Buttons

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Save token** | Saves the token as your live eq2codex.com token, replacing any token you had before, makes it the token the bot uses, and closes the window. The Codex tabs then show the token as configured.<br>*With an empty box nothing is saved and a red message says so.* |
| **Get a token at eq2codex.com ↗** | Opens eq2codex.com in your web browser, outside the game. Sign in there and go to Settings → API Tokens. |
| **Close** | Closes the window without saving. |
| **Title bar close (X)** | Closes the window without saving, the same as Close. |



## When a token is already saved

The status box gets a green border and says how many characters the saved token has. Paste a new token and save to replace it.

## What the window shows

- Status box: 'Live token: not set.' when you have no token yet, or 'Live token: configured (57 chars). Paste a new token to replace it.' when you do. The token itself is never shown.
- Stored in ... - the file your token is saved in: OgreSavePrivate\EQ2\Codex.json in your InnerSpace Scripts folder (your private settings folder - do not share it).

## Good to know

- The window closes by itself after a successful save. If the save fails, the window stays open with a red message saying why (for example, your token file cannot be read), and your token is still in the box.
- Saving replaces only the live token and makes it the one the bot uses. Anything else in Codex.json is kept.
- If Codex.json is damaged (cannot be read), the save is refused so nothing in it is lost. Fix or delete the file, then save again.

A save that could not be done - the red message says why, and the token you pasted is still in the box:

# Pushover Account

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Where you enter your two Pushover keys, so the bot can send alerts to your phone.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Get these at pushover.net** opens the site in your browser.
> - **Application API token** - from an application you create on pushover.net.
> - **Your user key** - from your Pushover dashboard.
> - **Save** stores both keys and clears the boxes. Both are needed - if one is missing, or the save fails, it says so in red and keeps what you typed.
> - **Send test** sends a test alert to your phone. The console says if it is on its way, or why not.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Your saved keys are never shown in full.
> - Which alerts are sent is set on the OgreBot Pushover tab. Its Disable boxes also block the test.

**Open it:** In OgreBot, open the **Pushover** tab and click **Set up Pushover account**.

## What it is for

Pushover is a phone app that the bot can use to send you alerts (for example, a tell, or a loot window left open). This window is where you enter your two Pushover keys: the API token of an application you create on pushover.net, and your own user key from your Pushover dashboard. Both are needed. The window never shows your saved keys in full - only their first 4 and last 2 characters.

## Your keys

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Application API token** | Paste the API token of the application you created on pushover.net. Pressing Enter is the same as Save.<br>*The grey text in an empty box is only an example of what a key looks like.* | empty |
| **Your user key** | Paste your user key from your pushover.net dashboard. Pressing Enter is the same as Save. | empty |



## Buttons

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Save** | Saves both keys, replacing any you had before, and the bot starts using them straight away. A green 'Saved.' appears, both boxes are cleared and the status box shows the keys as configured (in green). The window stays open.<br>*Both boxes must be filled - with one empty nothing is saved, a red message says which one is missing, and what you typed stays in the boxes. If the file cannot be written, a red message says so and your text stays too.* |
| **Send test** | Sends a test alert to your phone: 'Test from OgreBot. If you can read this, your Pushover details are working.' The console says whether it is on its way, or why it was not sent.<br>*It obeys the Pushover tab: with 'Disable ALL Pushover Messages' or 'Disable Normal Pushover Messages' ticked, the test is not sent.* |
| **Get these at pushover.net ↗** | Opens pushover.net in your web browser, outside the game. |
| **Close** | Closes the window. Anything typed but not saved is thrown away. |
| **Title bar close (X)** | Closes the window without saving, the same as Close. |



## After saving

The status box shows the first 4 and last 2 characters of each key, their length, and how many alerts are waiting to be sent.

## What the window shows

- Status box: 'pushover: NOT configured (...)' in yellow when either key is missing, or 'pushover: configured [app_token abcd...xy (30 chars), user_key abcd...xy (30 chars)], queue 0' in green when both are saved. 'queue' is the number of alerts waiting to go out.
- Stored in ... - the file your keys are saved in: OgreSavePrivate\Common\Pushover.json in your InnerSpace Scripts folder (your private settings folder - do not share it).

## Good to know

- Which alerts are sent is set on the OgreBot Pushover tab, not here.
