# Instance Controller

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Runs zones for your group, one after another: travels in, fights through the zone, and moves on to the next.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Queue zones**: pick a zone on the left and double-click it (or Add). **Add all** queues every zone the list is showing (this folder, after the search and the variant filter), in its order. Drag rows to reorder.
> - **Queue by name** from the console: `ogre ic queue add ${Zone.Name}` queues the zone you are in, and `ogre ic queue add Oogothl Sprawl: Ver'Bohten Atoll [Heroic]` any zone - its name with the difficulty, the way the game writes it.
> - **Sort** (beside the Zones heading): the zones are listed by their number by default (**Sort: #**); click it for **Sort: A-Z**, by zone name. It is remembered, and Add all follows it.
> - **Remove** and **Clear** sit beside the Queue heading. Remove takes out the highlighted row (click a row first; right-clicking the highlighted row does the same), Clear empties the queue. Each is greyed out until there is something for it to do.
> - **Stop after this zone** (shown while a list runs): the zone you are in finishes, then the list stops and the end-of-list actions run (call to the guild hall or home, camp). Run clears it.
> - **Run** starts the list. **Pause** holds the Instance Controller only (OgreBot keeps running). **End** stops the run. While the end-of-list actions run (guild hall, home, camp), End cancels them: nothing more starts, and the client is not closed.
> - **Options** decide what happens in and after each zone: loop the list, reset zones first, call home at the end, and more.
> - **Summon merc if solo** (on by default): once you are inside the zone with nobody else in your group, it summons your mercenary (`merc resume`). On a zone-in that happens as you land, so the mercenary gets the rebuff time too.
> - **Every option is also a command**: `ogre ic -skipshinies` turns one on, `ogre ic -skipshinies=off` turns it off, and one command can carry several. `ogre ic options` lists them all.
> - **Zone file**: IC File Option 1-3 and IC File Args are for the zone you run. The zone file names the options and reads them.
> - **Startup Options** are commands sent at the start of every zone, then undone when the run ends.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Closing this window stops a running list, the same as End.
> - If a zone fails, the group stays in that zone and the list stops.
> - Every zone file must say how it leaves the zone (a `zoneOut` step). A zone file with a problem still shows in the list, in red and marked "not valid": click it, and the bar under the list says what to fix. It cannot be queued until it is fixed.
> - While IC travels the group to a zone, OgreBot is paused, so buffs that drop at each zone line are not recast out in the world. A toon that gets attacked on the way is resumed to fight, then paused again. On arrival everyone is resumed and has a few seconds to rebuff before the zone starts.
> - Settings (including Max wait per zone) and the queue are saved, and shared by every character on this PC.

**Open it:** Type `ogre ic` (or `ogre ic panel`) in the console. It opens at 775x460. If the window is already open, the command does nothing.

## What it is for

The Instance Controller (IC) runs a list of instanced zones for you, one after another. You browse the zone files, queue the zones you want (each stamped with a variant such as [Heroic]), then press Run. For each row IC travels the group to the zone (resetting it first if asked), applies your Startup Options, walks the zone file's steps (paths, nameds, chests, shinies) and zones out, then moves to the next row. When the list is done it can call the group home, camp out, or loop back to the top.

## Run tab - options

When the window is too short for every option, the option grid scrolls (the lists and the footer stay on screen). The picture here is the grid as it opens; the next section's picture is the same grid scrolled down. Every tick-box is also a console switch, and `ogre ic options` lists them all.

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Loop list** | After the last row finishes, the list starts again at row 1. It is read again before each next row, so unticking it during a list ends the list at its last row. When a whole pass only skipped zones (every zone on the list locked), the list waits until the first lockout ends instead of going round again at once. It is turned OFF automatically when a safety check stops the list: fewer than 10 free inventory slots, the group shrank, or a member is dead.<br>*A zone never starts again inside the instance it just finished. If a zone's exit did not take the group out, the list stops and says why; Stop and Run starts it where you stand. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -loop` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Ogre IM** | At the end of a zone, at most once every 30 minutes, runs the Inventory Manager (`ogre im -destroy -tse`) and the list waits for it. It runs inside the zone, before the zone-out. With Call to GH after each zone, or when the list ends after this zone with At end of list: call to guild hall, it runs in the guild hall instead, where it also uses the depot and sells (`-depot -sell`), then the group repairs. The first one is due 30 minutes after the list starts. One that comes due while you are flying or dead is skipped until the next 30 minutes. Tick After each zone to run it after every zone instead.<br>*Only for a zone that ran (not one that was skipped), and not when Stop after this zone ends the list. A zone given up after a wipe gets it wherever the group then stands. If the IM is still going after 30 minutes, the list goes on without it; while it runs inside the zone, Max wait per zone does not count. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -im` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **After each zone** | With Ogre IM: the IM runs after every zone that ran, instead of at most once every 30 minutes. Everything else is as Ogre IM: what it runs, the guild hall's depot and sell, and the list waiting for it. Flying or dead after a zone: that zone is skipped, and the IM runs after the next one.<br>*Does nothing with Ogre IM off. Unticked during a list, the 30 minutes count from the last IM. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -imAfterEachZone` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Summon Mender bot to sell** | With Ogre IM, outside a guild hall: the IM also sells to a mender bot (`-sell`). A mender bot already within 10 m is used; otherwise, when a Mechanized Platinum Repository of Reconstruction is in your bags and ready, IC uses it to summon one. The bot is targeted so the sell picks it. The sell waits (up to a minute) for the automatic repair at the bot to end, because that repair closes the bot's window.<br>*No item, item not ready, or no bot 15 seconds after the summon: the IM runs without the sell, and the log says why. Does nothing in a guild hall (it has its own merchant) or with Ogre IM off. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -summonMenderToSell` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Zone in first** | When a row starts and you are not standing in its zone, the travel system takes the whole group there (the queued variant picks the instance), and the file starts on arrival. The group's bots are paused for the trip, so buffs that drop at each zone line are not recast out in the world. On arrival IC waits for the group (up to 2 minutes, then it names who is missing and goes on), resumes everyone and gives 8 seconds to rebuff before the file starts. If there is no route, IC warns and runs where you stand. Off = always runs where you stand.<br>*Zoning in always runs the file from the top; a named # or Resume point is dropped. Zone-in gives up after 20 minutes. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -zoneIn` (`=off` turns it off).* | ON |
| **Summon merc if solo** | Once inside the zone, when nobody else is in your group (your mercenary not out either), IC summons your mercenary (`merc resume`). On a zone-in it happens as the group lands, so the mercenary gets the rebuff time too; when you start already standing in the zone, it happens as the run starts.<br>*A character with no mercenary hired just gets the game's own one-line answer. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -summonMercIfSolo` (`=off` turns it off).* | ON |
| **Reset zones first** | Only during a zone-in. It first checks the lockout table (read again from the Zone Reuse window if the bot's copy is more than 15 minutes old; if it cannot be read within 30 seconds, the zone-in goes ahead without the check). Locked and not resettable: the zone is SKIPPED and the list moves on. Otherwise it runs 'oc !c -ResetZone igw:<you> <zone>' so every member resets, then travels. Off = lockouts are not checked; the group goes into the instance it is locked to.<br>*A locked zone that cannot be reset is skipped, not failed. This option never spends a Force reset charge. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -resetZones` (`=off` turns it off).* | ON |
| **Skip dead/missing checks** | The list does NOT stop when someone has left the group or a member is dead - checked when Run starts and before each next row. The free inventory slots check (at least 10) still stops it.<br>*Without it, either one stops the list, turns Loop list off and runs the end-of-list actions. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -skipGroupChecks` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Halt at end of zone** | When a zone's steps finish cleanly, IC pauses itself inside the zone, before the exit and zone-out steps. OgreBot keeps running. The Pause button becomes Resume; pressing it runs the zone-out and the list continues. If the group leaves the zone while held, the zone counts as done and IC stays paused until Resume.<br>*A zone file's own zone-out step can do the hold instead. That way also sends an OgreConsole message and speaks '<name> IC' aloud (text-to-speech). Not applied to a failed zone. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -haltAtEnd` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Call to GH after each zone** | When a zone's steps are done (after any Halt at end of zone), the group is called to the guild hall INSTEAD of zoning out: the group's bots are paused, you run GetToGH and the rest of the group is sent the same, and the bots are resumed when everyone has landed. Nothing of the zone file's exit runs; the next zone starts from the guild hall. A call that fails, or takes more than 180 seconds, ends the zone all the same (a carry-on failure, like a failed exit).<br>*When it is on, Stay until a zone is ready is not used. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -callGHAfterEachZone` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Stay until a zone is ready** | When a zone's steps are done, before the zone-out, the group waits INSIDE the zone until a zone still on the list can be run: not locked, or resettable. The zone you are in counts too: it is zoned out of, reset and run again. The lockout table is read once; when nothing is ready, it is read again when the first lockout among those zones ends. Untick it to let the group go.<br>*Not used when Call to GH after each zone is on, or when Stop after this zone is ticked (there is no next zone to wait for). Also a console switch: `ogre ic -stayInZoneUntilZoneReady` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **After a lost zone: Call to guild hall / Call to home / nothing** | When a zone is given up because all its wipe-recovery tries are used: everyone is revived first (up to 60 seconds), then this runs. 'Call to guild hall' = go to the guild hall (GetToGH), up to 180 seconds. 'Call to home' = cast Call to Home, up to 90 seconds. 'nothing' = revive only. The list then moves on to the next row.<br>*A zone file with its own after-wipe list replaces this completely. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -afterWipe=callgh` (or `callhome`, `none`).* | Call to guild hall (callgh) |
| **Skip the boss** | A named that the zone file marks as the boss is not fought. It ends as 'skipped - Do NOT Kill Boss is on'.<br>*Only affects nameds the zone file marks as the boss. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -skipBoss` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Skip shinies** | No shiny is looted at all: every shinies step of the zone file ends at once as 'skipped - the Skip shinies option is on'.<br>*Wins over whatever the zone file says about no-trade shinies. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -skipShinies` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Pause for no-trade shinies** | In a shinies step, a shiny whose loot window holds a no-trade item is NOT looted: its window is left open for you and IC pauses itself (the group keeps playing), with a console message and a '<name> shiny' text-to-speech. Take the item, then press Resume; the step goes on to the next shiny. Loot everything is turned off for the group while the step runs, so the auto-loot leaves the item too.<br>*A zone file that sets its own no-trade rule on the step overrides this option. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -pauseForNoTradeShinies` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |
| **Do not loot no-trade boss chest** | When the step for a named the zone file marks as the boss begins (before the kill), your Leader Only loot is set to take tradable items and Smart Loot only (not loot everything, no distribution, not in combat); your loot options are put back after the loot step. A Leader Only loot window that still holds a no-trade item is left open for you, with a console message and a '<name> boss chest' text-to-speech, and IC waits until it is gone.<br>*Free For All windows are not touched - each character's own loot options take them. Non-boss chests loot no-trade items as usual unless the zone file says otherwise. The zone file's own no-trade rule overrides this. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -doNotLootNoTradeBossChest` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |

## Run tab - bottom row

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **named #** | Type which named you are on (1, 2, 3...) and press Enter, or just press Run. Only works for the queue row whose zone you are standing in: that row's next run starts at that named's lead-in (its walk and pre-fight steps). The row shows 'start at step N (named X)'. Used once. 1 or blank = from the top. If you are not in a queued zone, it is ignored with a warning.<br>*The box empties once it is used. A file with no such named is refused.* | blank |

The zone you are in finishes as always (its steps, then Halt at end of zone or Call to GH after each zone), then the list stops as if that were its last row - no Ogre IM, no loop wait, no next zone, no Stay until a zone is ready - and the end-of-list actions run. Untick it to carry on.<br>*Run clears it, and so does the list stopping. Not saved. A failed zone still stops the list without the end-of-list actions. Also a console switch: `ogre ic -stopAfterZone` (`=off` turns it off).* | off |

Run also clears the pause.* | running |
| **Resume** | Only shown when you stand in a queued zone whose last run was stopped mid-zone. That row restarts at the step where it stopped, then Run fires.<br>*The stop point is not saved to disk.* | hidden |

Each row then runs in turn: zone-in, apply startup options, zone steps, exit.<br>*Greyed out while running, while the end-of-list actions of the last list are still going (press End to cancel them first), with an empty queue, or if OgreBot is not loaded with a profile. The console says what was left behind.<br>*Greyed out only when nothing is running and no end-of-list action is running. | Control | What it does | Default |

## Good to know

- Closing the IC window ends the running list, exactly like pressing End. It does not just hide the window.
- Run starts at the queue row for the zone you are standing in, otherwise at row 1. The highlighted row does not decide where Run starts.
- Pausing IC does not pause OgreBot, and pausing OgreBot does not stop IC. A paused OgreBot with IC running is the normal way to move through a zone.
- The Difficulty setting is saved on the row but a run does not use it yet.
- When the window is short, the options scroll: scroll them down for At end of list and Zone file.
- A failed zone stops the list and leaves the group in that zone so it can be looked at. No end-of-list action (guild hall, home, camp) runs.
- A zone lost to a wipe is different: everyone is revived, the After a lost zone choice runs, and the list moves on.
- End-of-list actions run only when the list completes or a safety check stops it (fewer than 10 free bag slots, someone left the group, or someone is dead). Never after End.
- End cancels the end-of-list actions while they run. Anything not yet started never runs and an unfinished camp is stopped (the client is not closed); a trip to the guild hall or a Call to Home already under way finishes on its own. Closing the window does not cancel them.
- Camp out camps the whole group, not just the IC character.
- Zoning in always runs the zone file from the top. A named # or Resume point only applies when you are already standing in that zone.
- Reload profile when the run ends (on by default) reloads the profile of every group member at the end of each zone. It also throws away any change you made by hand during the run.
- The search text is not saved.
- All IC settings (including Max wait per zone), the queue and the startup list live in one file, Scripts/OgreSave/EQ2/IC/panel.json, shared by every character on the PC. Each session loads it once and overwrites it on every change.
- Refresh (Zone lockouts) does not read the lockout table straight away. It is re-read the next time a zone-in needs it.
- Add defaults appends the shipped rows again, so pressing it twice gives duplicates. Reset to defaults and both Clear buttons ask no confirmation.

# Zone Reset

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Shows your zone lockout timers and resets them, for this character or for your group or fleet.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Refresh** - read the zone list and timers from the game again.
> - **Click a zone** on the left to queue it; click its **x** on the right to remove it. The **Add** buttons queue many at once.
> - **Show** - tick Solo, Heroic, Raid or Other to choose which zones the list shows. **Sort** - by Status (resettable first), Type (solo, heroic, raid) or Name. These only change the view: the **Add** buttons still look at every zone.
> - **Reset queue for me** - reset every queued zone on this character.
> - **Reset queue via ... for ...** - send the reset to the characters in the Master Control Panel's For Who window.
> - **Force reset** - click a queued zone to select it, then press it twice: the first press only arms it, a second press within 5 seconds does the reset.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Green can be reset, orange is locked (time left in brackets), yellow has no data.
> - **Force reset spends a limited reset charge** and cannot be undone. It only confirms if the game's message names that zone.

**Open it:** Type `ogre zone` (also `ogre reset` or `ogre zonereset`). If the window is already open, this does nothing. `ogre end zone` (or the window's X) stops a read or a reset this window started and closes the window.

## What it is for

Zone Reset shows your zone lockout timers and resets them for you. It reads the game's Zone Reuse window and lists every zone, coloured by whether it can be reset now, is still locked, or has no data. You pick the zones you want in a reset queue, then reset them on this character, or send the reset to your group or fleet. A separate Force reset button spends one of your limited reset charges on one zone. It needs two clicks, and has a safety check.

## Header and colours

The status text on the right says what the last action did (for example 'scraped 7 zone(s) + 3 with no timer'). 'working…' appears next to the title while it is busy. The colour key explains the zone list: green can be reset, orange is locked with the time left shown in brackets, pale yellow has no data.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Refresh** | Reads the zone list and timers again. It briefly opens the game's Zone Reuse window, reads it, and closes it.<br>*Opening this window with no list yet does a Refresh for you. Nothing happens when you are not in the world.* |

## Full zone list

Every zone the game has a lockout record for: resettable first, then locked, then no data. Zones with 'Raid' in the name carry a small 'raid' tag. Hover a row to see the game's own tooltip for it.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Click a zone** | Adds that zone to the reset queue on the right. A zone already in the queue is not added twice. |
| **Add resettable** | Adds every green (resettable) zone to the queue. |
| **Add non-raid** | Adds every resettable zone that does not have 'Raid' in its name. |
| **Add non-locked** | Adds every zone that is not locked: the resettable ones and the ones with no data. |

|---|---|---|

| **x on a queued zone** | Removes it from the queue. |  |
| **Clear list** | Empties the reset queue. |  |
| **Force reset selected / Force reset: <zone> / Click again to SPEND a charge on: <zone>** | Spends one of your limited zone reset charges to reset the selected zone right now, even if it is locked. It takes two clicks: the first click only arms the button (its text changes to 'Click again to SPEND a charge on: <zone>'), and a second click within 5 seconds does the reset. A reminder of how the lists work, and that Force reset spends a limited charge.

## What the window shows

- 'raid' tag: the zone name contains 'Raid'. The Add non-raid button skips these.
- Status text (top right): what the last action did, for example 'reset 3 queued zone(s)', 'the reset queue is empty', 'not in world', or 'ABORTED: ...' when the Force reset safety check refused.

## Good to know

- Closing the window stops a read of the zone list, or a reset, that this window (or `ogre zone`) started, the same as `ogre end zone`. One the Instance Controller or a script asked for keeps going.

- Refresh and Force reset briefly open the game's Zone Reuse window. That can cause a short stutter. A queue reset opens it too, but only on a character whose game has not loaded its zone list yet.

# NPC Cast Monitoring

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Two tabs. **Monitor** writes down every spell a mob casts, with its name and spell number. **Watch list** watches the mobs listed for your current zone, and when one starts a listed spell, the bot can interrupt it or run your commands.
>
> **Monitor**
>
> - **Start** / **Stop** - turns the log on and off. It keeps running while you fight, zone or die, until you press Stop or close the window (closing it is the same as `ogre end npccast`).
> - **my target + its target** - logs your target and your target's target. These are the only spells that come with a **name**, because the name is read off your cast bars. Target the mob, or target the tank the mob is attacking.
> - **+ named NPCs nearby** / **+ every NPC nearby** - also logs other casters within 100 metres. Their spells show a spell number but no name, unless you have seen that spell on your cast bar before.
> - **Copy** - puts the whole list on your clipboard, ready to paste.
> - **Clear** - empties the list for this zone.
> - Each zone has its own list, saved to the file named under it. Send that file, or paste the copied list, to whoever asked for it.
>
> **Watch list**
>
> - **Add the spell my target is casting** - press it while your target's cast bar is up.
> - **Add by hand** - type a mob name and a spell number.
> - **interrupt** - the bot interrupts that spell with an interrupt you have ready.
> - **label** / **commands** - opens a small editor under the spell. Type, then **Save** or **Cancel**.
> - **remove** / **remove mob** - stop watching a spell or a whole mob here.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - The Watch list only works when **NPC Cast Monitoring** is ticked on OgreBot's Settings tab. While it is off, a red bar tells you when a button cannot do anything. Interrupts and commands also need OgreBot open and not paused. The Monitor needs none of that.

> - Your changes go in your own list, so updates never remove them. **Reset my edits** deletes them all for this zone, with no undo.

| **label** | Opens a small editor right under this spell's row, with a box holding its current name. Type a new name and press Save. Cancel closes the editor without saving.<br>*Saving an empty box removes the name you gave it. A spell from the shipped list then shows its shipped name again.* |  |
| **commands** | Opens a small editor right under this spell's row, with the commands the bot runs each time this mob starts this spell, one per line. The row shows how many commands are set and what they are.<br>*Commands only run while OgreBot is open and not paused.* |  |



## Watch list: editing a label or commands

Pressing label or commands opens this editor under the spell's row.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Editor box** | The name or the command lines you are editing. What you type is kept while the list refreshes. Only one editor is open at a time; opening another one drops what you had not saved.<br>*Pressing Enter does not save. Use the Save button.* |
| **Save** | Saves the editor's text into your own list for this zone and closes the editor. For commands, empty lines are skipped, and saving an empty box removes your commands; a spell from the shipped list then uses its shipped commands again. |
| **Cancel** | Closes the editor. Nothing is saved. |
| **remove** | Removes this spell from the mob. A spell from your own list is deleted. A shipped spell is switched off in your list.<br>*Removing the last spell of a mob you added removes that mob too. A mob from the shipped list stays watched.* |



## Watch list: footer

Shows how far the bot scans and for what (NPCs or players), and where the shipped list and your own list for this zone are stored on disk.

## What the window shows

- Monitor header: the zone the log belongs to - the same name the Watch list tab shows - and 'N spell(s) logged', the number of rows in the log.
- Monitor log columns: Mob, Spell (its name, or a grey '(no name yet)'), Ability ID, Cast time (seconds, from the latest cast) and Seen (how many times that mob cast it). One cast counts once, even though the Monitor sees it several times while the cast bar is up. Casting the same spell again counts again.
- Monitor grey bars: the result of your last Start, Stop, Copy or Clear. With either 'nearby' choice, a second bar says that casts from mobs that are not your target (or your target's target) only get an ability id.
- Monitor footer: 'monitoring' in green while it runs, 'stopped' when it does not, and 'saved to' the log file for this zone.
- Zone name (both tabs): the file name used for the zone, for example Castle_Vacrul_Caverns_of_the_Forsaken_Heroic_II. '(no zone)' when nothing is loaded.
- 'N mob(s) · N ability entr(ies) · watching N': how many mobs and spells are listed, and how many mobs the scan is watching right now.
- Green bar: your last capture worked (or that spell was already in your list). Red bar: your last capture or edit did not work, with the reason (no target, not casting, monitoring off, radius out of range, and so on).
- Red 'OFF' bar: NPC Cast Monitoring is turned off on OgreBot's Settings tab. The list is empty and nothing is watched.
- Grey 'OgreBot is closed' bar: mobs are still watched, but nothing is interrupted and no commands run until OgreBot is open.
- Grey 'Per-cast logging is off' bar: shows until you tick NPCCastDebug on OgreBot's Debug tab.
- Red file bars: the shipped list or your list for this zone cannot be read, your file uses the old 'nomerge' spelling, or some spell numbers in it are not valid and were skipped.
- Grey 'REPLACES' bar: your file is set to replace the shipped list instead of adding to it.
- Mob tags: 'yours' = from your list, 'radius N' = a distance set just for that mob, 'watch only' = no spells listed.
- Spell rows: the spell number, its label ('(no label)' if none), the interrupt box, how many commands it runs, and a 'yours' tag when it comes from your list.
- Watch list footer: 'scanning to N for NPCs/players' is the widest distance in use (the zone radius or the biggest per-mob radius). 'shipped:' and 'yours:' are the two list files for this zone.

## Good to know

- The Monitor and the Watch list are separate. The Monitor only writes down what mobs cast; it never interrupts or runs anything, and it needs neither the NPC Cast Monitoring checkbox nor OgreBot open.
- Spell names in the Monitor only come from your cast bars (your target and your target's target), and only when the cast lasts longer than about a quarter of a second. Once a spell's name is known, it shows for that spell everywhere, in every zone and next time too.
- Each zone keeps its own Monitor log. Zone while it runs and the old zone's log is saved and the new zone's saved log comes back. Send the file named under the log, or paste what Copy gives you.
- Closing the window stops the Monitor, the same as `ogre end npccast`. Stop (or `ogre npccast monitor off`) stops it and keeps the window open. The console can also start it: `ogre npccast monitor`, `ogre npccast monitor named` or `ogre npccast monitor all`; it then runs without the window until you stop it.
- The Watch list is switched by the 'NPC Cast Monitoring' checkbox on OgreBot's 'Settings' tab. While it is off, the list is empty and capturing, reloading, adding, removing and editing are refused with a red bar saying so. Only Import still works.
- Entering a zone that has a list (shipped or yours) ticks that checkbox for you, once per zone. If you untick it, it stays off until you next enter a zone that has a list, or your profile is loaded again.
- A watched mob starting a spell that is not listed (or a 'watch only' mob) does not interrupt or run anything. It only lets encounter scripts know the mob is casting.
- Interrupts and commands only happen while OgreBot is open and not paused.
- Reset my edits deletes all your Watch list edits for this zone at once, with no confirmation.
- The Watch list only covers the zone you are standing in. To edit another zone's list, go there or edit its file.
- With NPC Cast Monitoring off, an empty Watch list says so and asks you to turn it on - adding needs it on.

# Effect Tracker

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Records what effects are called and what they say they do, into a shared per-zone data set for encounter writers.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Player** - record the detrimentals that land on you.
> - **NPC** - record the effects on NPCs that are fighting.
> - **Merge zone** - combine every character's capture file for the zone you are in into one. It works with Player and NPC off too.
> - **Search** and click a row to read everything recorded for an effect.
> - **Right-click a row** to copy its coding details.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Both start off in every new session. The window opens shrunk; click **Expand**.
> - Each effect is examined once, ever, so a zone you ran before may show nothing new.
> - It switches itself off while the Encounter Recorder runs.

**Open it:** Type `ogre effecttracker` (or `ogre oet`). `ogre effecttracker on` turns on the player capture and `ogre effecttracker npc on` the NPC capture; both also open the window. `ogre effecttracker off` turns both off. `ogre end effecttracker` (or the window's X) turns both off and closes the window.

## What it is for

Effect Tracker records what effects are called and what they say they do. With Player on, it records every detrimental that lands on you. With NPC on, it records the effects on NPCs that are fighting. It opens each new effect's examine window, copies its text, and closes it again. Each effect is only looked at once, ever, and the results go into a shared per-zone data set that encounter writers use. This window turns the capture on and off and lets you browse what was captured in the zone you are in.

## Header

The green line shows the zone, your character, how many effects are waiting to be examined, and the name of the last effect captured.

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Shrink / Expand** | Shrink folds the window down to a small title bar with just an Expand button, so it is out of your way. Expand brings it back to the size it was before you shrank it (940 by 600 the first time, because the window opens shrunk). Display only - the capture keeps running while shrunk.<br>*The window always opens shrunk. Click Expand to see the list.* | shrunk (the window opens small) |

## Turning the capture on and off

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Player: off / Player: ON** | Turns on (or off) recording of the detrimentals that land on you. It is not saved: it starts off again in every new session. Turning off the last of Player and NPC saves what was captured straight away.* | off |
| **NPC: off / NPC: ON** | Turns on (or off) recording of the effects on NPCs and named NPCs that have a target (are fighting). Up to 10 effects per NPC.<br>*It only captures during combat, because that is when NPCs carry the effects.* | off |
| **Merge zone** | Combines every character's capture file for the zone you are in into one merged file for the zone. Your own latest captures are saved first, so they are included. The console says how many records were added and updated.<br>*It works whether Player or NPC is on or not. Outside a zone it says 'not in a zone' and does nothing.* |  |

## Browsing what was captured

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Player dets (N)** | Shows the detrimentals your character has recorded in this zone. The number is how many there are. Display only. | selected |
| **NPC effects (N)** | Shows the NPC effects recorded in this zone (shared by all your characters). Display only. |  |
| **search name / icon key / effect text / actor (box)** | Shows only the effects whose name, description, icon key, effect text or mob names contain what you type. Capital letters do not matter. It searches after you stop typing or press Enter. Display only. | empty |
| **Clear** | Empties the search box and shows everything again. Display only. |  |

## The list

One row per captured effect: its name, its icon key (two numbers), how many lines of text it has, '+desc' if it has a description, 'on <mob>' for NPC effects, any crowd control it causes in yellow, and a red note if the capture failed.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Click a row** | Opens the row to show everything recorded for that effect: icon numbers, duration, counters, the description, each line of effect text, and the mobs it was seen with (named mobs are starred). Click it again to close it. Display only.<br>*Dragging across text selects it for copying instead of opening the row.* |
| **Right-click a row > Copy coding details** | Copies the effect's name, icon numbers, duration, the mob it was on, and all its text to the Windows clipboard, ready to paste into an encounter file or a note.<br>*If another program is holding the clipboard, the console says it could not copy.* |

## Footer

Record count (and 'showing N of M' when there are more than 250 matches; the file still has them all), how many effects were captured this session, how many effects are already known and will never be examined again, how many examine windows were opened, any timeouts or windows that were not ours, and how many server reads were used.

## What the window shows

- Header line: zone file name, your character, 'N/8 examining' while effects wait to be read, and 'last:' the last effect captured.
- Red 'Disarmed' bar: the Encounter Recorder or one of the old OgreBot effect tracker scripts is running. They use the same examine window, so the tracker switches itself off and refuses to turn on until they stop.

- While the tracker is working, you will see effect examine windows flash open and close. That is how it reads them.
- The list only shows the zone you are in, and only after capture has been turned on (or Merge zone pressed) this session.

# Hide Effects

> **:bulb: In game - the ? button on this window says**
>
> Hides clutter (guild buffs, pets, always-on class buffs) from your character's effects window, using lists of effect names.
>
> **What you can do here**
>
> - **Run** - check the effects on you and hide every one on a list for your class.
> - **List hidden**, **Clear all**, **Un-hide id** - see what the game is hiding, or show it again.
> - **Add** / **remove** - change the names in a group.
> - **Reset my edits** - undo every Add and remove you made, in all groups.
>
> **Good to know**
>
> - Nothing is hidden until you press **Run**. Effects that land later need another Run.

**Open it:** Type `ogre hideeffects panel`. If the window is already open, this does nothing. Plain `ogre hideeffects` does not open the window: it runs the hide straight away (the same as the Run button). `ogre end hideeffects` (or the window's X) stops a run and closes the window; effects already hidden stay hidden.

## What it is for

Hide Effects clears clutter out of your character's effects window. It keeps lists of effect names that nobody needs to see (guild buffs, pets, always-on class buffs and so on). When you press Run, it looks at every effect on your character and tells the game to hide the ones whose name is on a list that applies to you. The lists are split into groups: some apply to everyone, some only to one class or archetype. Your own additions and removals are kept in a separate file, so bot updates never undo them.

## Header

Shows the character, class and archetype the lists are matched against. 'run in progress…' appears on the right while a run is working.

## Buttons

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Run** | Checks every effect on your character, one at a time, and hides each one whose name is on a list that applies to your class. A list name also matches the same name with a rank after it (for example 'Aspect of Genius' also hides 'Aspect of Genius IV'). The console says what it checked and what it hid.<br>*It only runs when you press it; it does not keep hiding new effects afterwards. | **effect id (box)** | The id of one hidden effect, as shown by List hidden. Anything that is not a digit is removed. | empty |
| **Un-hide id** | Tells the game to show that one hidden effect again. Nothing happens if the box is empty. |  |
| **Reload list** | Reads all the hide lists from disk again. Use it after editing a list file by hand. |  |
| **Reset my edits** | Deletes the file that holds your Add and remove changes, so every group loses those changes at once. A list file you put in your own custom folder by hand is not touched.<br>*No confirmation and no undo.* |  |

One block per group, with the groups that apply to you first. | Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Group name / arrow** | Click to fold a group away or open it again. Display only - changes what you see, not what the bot does. | open |

Left: how many groups and names apply to your character. | **updates (slider)** | How many times a second the radar re-reads the game, 1 to 30. More gives fresher positions but costs more game time. This is the only setting in the drawer that costs anything.<br>*Not saved by Save: it goes back to 5 when the extension reloads.* | 5 / sec |
| **updates: +** | Reads the game one time more per second (up to 30). |  |
| **chunk: − / +** | Steps through off, 25, 50, 100, 150, 250, 400. When more actors are around than this number, one update is spread over several game frames, which smooths out the worst frame but makes positions a little older.<br>*Not saved by Save. With 100 things or fewer around, the default 100 does nothing, so there is no need to turn it off. The hint next to it says the same.* | 100 /frame |
| **glide** | Slides dots smoothly between updates instead of letting them jump. Costs the game nothing. | on |
| **blip size** | Size of each dot, 4 to 18 pixels. Named mobs are drawn 3 pixels bigger. | 8px |
| **label size** | Size of the name text, 7 to 20 pixels. | 9.5px |
| **background** | The same setting as dim on the toolbar: 0% is fully see-through (the radar is still clickable), 100% solid. | 94% |
| **auto-hide** | Turns clean mode on or off. In clean mode the title bar is removed and the toolbar fades out; hover the top-left corner of the radar to bring the controls back. See Clean mode below. | off |
| **click-through** | When ON, your mouse clicks go through the radar to the game, except on the dots and the toolbar, which still work.<br>*If you get stuck, `ogre radar click off` gives the mouse back and `ogre radar settings` opens this drawer.* | off |



## Settings - Full screen

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Full screen: on / off** | Stretches the radar over the whole game screen, minus the offsets below. Turning it on also turns click-through on, so you can still play. Turning it off puts the window back where it was. | off |
| **offsets px: top / right / bottom / left** | Pixels to leave free at each edge of the screen when full screen is on (for example to keep your hot bars clear).<br>*Typing a number does nothing until you press apply.* | 0 |
| **apply** | Re-fits the full-screen radar using the offsets you typed.<br>*Only does something while full screen is on.* |  |



## Settings - Colours

One row per kind of thing (by type) and one per con colour. Click a swatch to open the colour picker above the list.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Swatch (coloured dot)** | Opens the colour picker for that row. The picker's heading uses the row's own name (named, craft, nokill...). |
| **#hex box** | Type a colour as # plus six hex digits (for example #ff8a3d). It applies as soon as the text is a valid colour.<br>*Anything else is ignored.* |
| **Picker: #hex box** | The picked colour as text; type a colour here to set it. |

## Settings - Lists

Each entry is part of a name and is matched without caring about capitals: 'darkpaw' matches 'a Darkpaw brute'. Saved lists are kept when you press Save. Temporary lists are never saved; they last until the game's UI reloads.

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **whitelist ON / off (saved)** | When ON, only things whose name matches an entry are drawn.<br>*An empty whitelist is ignored rather than hiding everything.* | off |
| **blacklist ON / off (saved)** | When ON, things whose name matches an entry are hidden. | off |
| **whitelist ON / off (temporary)** | Same as the saved whitelist, for this session only. | off |
| **blacklist ON / off (temporary)** | Same as the saved blacklist, for this session only. | off |
| **name fragment, then Enter or Add (placeholder)** | Type part of a name. Enter does the same as Add. | empty |
| **Add** | Adds the typed text to that list (skipped if it is already there). |  |
| **✕ (remove entry)** | Removes that entry from the list. |  |



## Settings - Debug, Save and Reset

The report buttons print to the console, not the radar. They are for tracking down problems.

| Control | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **readout (shown / hidden)** | Shows a line at the top of the radar: how many things are drawn out of how many were read, and what the radar costs the game (time per frame and share of the CPU).<br>*Messages for you use the same line and show even when the readout is hidden: "no data for N s" when the radar has had nothing from the game for over 3 seconds, and what a clicked dot targeted.* | hidden |
| **verify cache** | Same as the verify button on the toolbar. |  |
| **perf** | Prints the radar's cost report to the console. |  |
| **status** | Prints the radar's current settings and state to the console. |  |
| **ui dump** | Prints the radar window's own drawing numbers to the console. |  |
| **dump** | Prints the first 10 things from the last update (name, type, distance, level, health, con, position) to the console. |  |
| **fields: full** | Reads everything the radar can show, including level, health and con. The lit button is the one in use. | full |
| **fields: lean** | Stops reading level, health and con: fewer game reads, but those three stop updating on the radar.<br>*Not saved. It goes back to full when the extension reloads.* |  |
| **reset perf** | Zeroes the radar's cost counters so the next perf report starts from now. |  |
| **force a scan** | Makes the radar re-read the game once, right away. |  |

| **Reset all** | Puts every setting in the drawer back to the defaults, including zoom, dim, height, colours, the type choices and the saved lists (they are emptied). Nothing is written until you press Save.<br>*Close and reopen the radar without saving to get your saved settings back.* |  |

## Clean mode (auto-hide)

With auto-hide on, the radar becomes a clean overlay: the title bar is gone (so no X, lock or ?) and the toolbar fades out. The space stays reserved at the top. A faint corner mark in the top-left shows where to point.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Hot corner (top-left)** | Point the mouse at the top-left corner of the radar (about 130 by 24 pixels) and the toolbar comes back. Move away and it fades again after a moment.<br>*The controls stay up while the settings drawer is open.* |
| **? (beside the gear)** | Opens this help. It only appears in clean mode, because the title bar and its ? are gone. |



## What the window shows

- Your character: the white dot with the cyan ring in the middle. The cyan wedge is the way you face.
- Dot colours (colour by type, the default): npc orange, named orange and bigger with a glow, nokill (guards, merchants) grey, special yellow, players blue, pets light green, your pet bright green, mercenaries teal, corpses dark grey, craft (crafting stations) purple, resources (harvest nodes) teal-green, chests amber, doors brown, anything else grey. Every colour can be changed in the settings.
- Dot shapes: players and mercenaries are rounded squares; everything else is round. Named mobs are 3 pixels bigger with an orange glow.
- Faded dots: corpses and anything at 0% health.
- Your target: its dot turns white with a white halo and its name goes white and bold.
- Rings: a third, two thirds and all of the zoom distance, measured to the nearer edge of the window. A tall window sees further up and down than sideways.
- Top right of the toolbar: the zone you are in.
- Only the 250 nearest things are drawn.

## Good to know

- Zoom, dim, height and every setting in the drawer are only kept for good if you press Save. Close and reopen the radar without saving and they go back to what you last saved (if you have never saved, your changes last until the game's UI reloads). Exception: choices in the Radar Types window save themselves. Only the type choice is saved - your other changes still wait for Save.
- updates and chunk are not saved at all; they return to 5 per second and 100 when the extension reloads.
- In clean mode there is no title bar and no X. Use the hot corner to get the controls back, or type `ogre end radar` to close it.
- If the radar stops you clicking the game, turn click-through on. If you then cannot reach the radar's settings, type `ogre radar settings`; `ogre radar click off` gives the mouse back, and `ogre radar uireset` puts every radar setting back to default (like Reset all - not saved until you press Save).
- Full screen turns click-through on for you.
- `ogre radar` when the radar is already open does nothing and prints nothing.
